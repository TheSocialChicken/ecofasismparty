Ecotopia — Party Architecture & Design

**Current as of Sept 30, 2026\.** This is the single up-to-date version of the full design — premise, guest journey, and mechanics — synthesized from the concept development, the Sept 9 kickoff, the design committee's Sept 10 and Sept 15 sessions, and the most recent games/activities brainstorm session. It's meant to be read start to finish as "what the party actually is" right now. Open questions, half-formed ideas, and the ongoing brainstorm trail live separately in the committee working doc — that's the working scratchpad; this is the current answer.

# **The premise**

The outside world got wrecked. Somewhere in a collapsed, burning, non-ecological wasteland, word has spread of one place that still works: **Ecotopia**, the last sustainable utopian country left. People apply to join it.

What they don't know is that Ecotopia is an extremely fascist-run state. Structurally it's fascism by the book — corporations and religion team up to override government, religion supplies "we are one with the earth, we must sacrifice," corporations supply forced-efficiency labor framed as service. Tonally it's 1984, but the "war" is against climate collapse rather than a foreign enemy — it needs to stay fun and a little absurd throughout, never a lecture.

**The one hard rule everything else follows: all in-universe messaging is relentlessly positive, even when it's describing something horrible.** Nobody is sent to prison — they receive "the privilege of being assigned to the recycling plant." Every rule, punishment, and piece of propaganda gets the same treatment. This is what makes the fascism legible as fascism only from the outside, exactly as intended.

Guest roles mirror the roughly 90/5/5 split of Dutch WWII occupation: most guests are ordinary citizens complying for carbon credits and having a normal fun party, a minority become active collaborators, and another minority become the secret resistance.

The story's actual moral only lands at the very end, through the resistance: the games, the ministries, the whole credit system turn out to be a distraction from real sustainability, existing mainly to manufacture compliance and power. Genuine environmental intention and connection to nature — not optimizing a number — is what the resistance actually stands for, and its own messaging deliberately never uses the words "eco" or "sustainable" to make that point without saying it outright. The same tension shows up in smaller jokes throughout the night — e.g. tallying how much water a single AI chatbot prompt costs — the same trap of "this technology will save us" running alongside its real footprint, which is also where the app's own "AI is banned in Ecotopia" easter egg comes from.

# **The guest journey**

**1\. Application, before the party.** Guests apply to join Ecotopia through a website — built to look and feel beautiful and magical, not sinister. The application collects the inputs used to calculate each applicant's starting carbon debt. One of the questions is a version of the AI-water joke above (something like "how many AI prompts do you send a day"), translated into an equivalent amount of water — funny on its own, and a preview of the AI-is-banned gag that shows up elsewhere in the app.

**2\. Arrival and induction, in the garage.** Confirmed: the garage is the entrance, not the second entrance — it has room for roughly double the guests (about 20 at once versus 10\) and already feels separated from the outside world the moment you're in it. The plan is to build the whole entrance into a maze of small staged moments, bigger in scope than any previous year: guests come down a ramp into a tunnel made of draped fabric (the reference point is a New Year's party a few years back that did the same trick with the roof and walls), which does the job of making the descent feel like dropping into a different level of reality before anyone's even reached registration. Guests who completed the application move through a faster induction, since their debt is already known. Guests who didn't get pulled into an interrogation room — small, dimly lit, one chair in the middle, two officers running a good cop / bad cop carbon-credit audit on the spot. Intimidating and a bit chaotic, on purpose. Either way, everyone arrives at "the ministry" — a registration desk staffed more elaborately than a normal door check, meant to feel like walking into an actual government office — presents their application confirmation (or gets interrogated), and leaves with their carbon debt passport and a ministry assignment.

**3\. The passport booklet.** Every guest receives one on entry. It records their assigned ministry and their carbon debt, and it's where credits get tallied through the rest of the night.

**4\. Working off carbon debt.** Before contributing to their ministry, every citizen has to pay down their starting debt through a genre of deliberately weird, silly carbon-reduction tasks — its own open design space, not yet built out into a concrete list, but see Games & activities below for the current candidate pool.

**5\. Ministry life.** Once debt-free, a citizen starts actually contributing to their ministry. Ministries take their names from sustainability symbols rather than generic departments (a draft list is below) — deliberately not a consistent taxonomy, mixing vegetables with elements, animals, and abstract categories, because a slightly-off classification system reads funnier than a tidy one. Each ministry has to figure out who's in it, what it's about, and what it's trying to achieve — and it's competitive: every ministry is trying to generate the most power as efficiently as possible while staying sustainable, which turns out to be genuinely hard. That difficulty pushes ministries into real dilemmas: overusing resources, or making inhumane calls that force people into things they don't want to do — always still framed positively. That tension is the actual engine of the ministry-play stage.

**6\. Escalating rules.** Each ministry starts with a handful of baseline rules, worded positively, designed to get strangers doing silly things together. Roughly once an hour, the ruling party adds more on top — a live escalation rather than a fixed rulebook handed out once, delivered through the same speaker system that runs the party's constant surveillance atmosphere (see Voice and messaging below).

**7\. Individual quests.** On top of ministry play, every guest also gets a personal quest. A small number of these — roughly ten — are the seeding mechanism for the guests meant to go looking for the resistance; nobody else can tell which quest pool anyone else got theirs from.

**8\. The recycling plant.** Guests who aren't adhering to doctrine — not sustainable enough, not religious enough — get sent here, framed as a privilege rather than a punishment ("promoted," not punished). The current idea for what actually happens there: guests get put through some kind of upcycling process — part boot-camp physicality, part ritual — and come back out with their carbon debt reset to zero and a new name, having shed their old, insufficiently-sustainable identity. It doubles as a second place resistance clues get seeded, alongside the moles.

**9\. Finding the resistance.** Hidden in a bar in Thijs's room. Not advertised — findable through curiosity, rumor, the pre-briefed mole cadre, individual quests, and clues seeded at the recycling plant. Membership spreads through a single physical object: one stamp, carried by whoever's currently "in," passed hand to hand — you can only join by being stamped by someone who's already a member, and the stamp goes somewhere inconspicuous (the ankle is the current idea) so it's visible to anyone who knows to look and invisible to anyone who doesn't.

**10\. The revolution.** The "game" portion of the night runs roughly two to two and a half hours. It builds toward a staged public moment — the regime's leader making an arrival, with smoke, sound, and a call for every ministry to gather and show what they've built (a song, a chant, a formation) as if for judgment. Ministries have been told all along to be "as ready as possible" for this moment without ever being told exactly what it involves, so they show up over-prepared for something vague. That's also the trigger point: once the resistance has pulled in more than half the party, this is where they move — the gathering crowd, and the spectacle built to hold it, becomes what the revolution raids. As it builds, cracks start showing in the official story — the regime has been lying about something (exactly what is still being decided; candidates are in the committee working doc) — and part of what's revealed is that the games and system were never really about sustainability, just about compliance and manufacturing power. Guests who've found their way into the resistance try to build a working alternative — genuinely difficult, full of real dilemmas — so governing well turns out to be much harder than complying ever was. Whether the revolution succeeds is left open. What's certain: it's against the state and its control, not against sustainability itself. This is the party's narrative ending; the party itself keeps going afterward as a normal dance party, DJs taking over the energy the revolution built. Current runtime plan: doors around 9pm, DJs through roughly 4am, with a possible extension to 6am in a second room if that room's owner is on board.

# **Core mechanics at a glance**

**Carbon credits.** One currency, not two: every guest starts with a negative balance (their carbon debt, set at application or in the interrogation room), and everything from there — working off debt, ministry contribution, winning games — moves the same number upward. Earning credits is also how you accumulate power and privilege; the game deliberately doesn't explain why being more sustainable also makes you more powerful, since that disorienting realization is part of the point.

**The passport booklet.** The physical object tying it all together: ministry, carbon debt/credit balance, and a running tally, carried by every guest all night. Crossing a threshold unlocks privileges — better drinks, access to certain rooms (see VIP room below), advancing to the next stage.

**VIP room.** Guests with a cleared debt and enough accumulated credit get access to a dedicated room — currently planned for the library — serving something like a proper scotch, reserved for "powerful" members. Deliberately modeled on how political parties reward their own hierarchy.

**Singles and family status.** Optional and self-reported only: a guest can signal they're single (a different-colored passport or string is the current idea), which quietly earns bonus credits — "not contributing to overpopulation." A guest who mentions having kids takes a small debt penalty in the other direction. Nobody is required to disclose either.

**Bar duty.** Running the bar is folded into the game as a civic task rather than a separate volunteer ask: one pre-assigned, experienced lead per shift, plus a guest pulled in on the spot to work alongside them. Shifts run roughly 45 minutes to an hour, covering the first three hours of the night while the game is live; after that, bar coverage continues informally by asking whoever's still around and willing.

**Ministries.** Competing to generate the most power as efficiently as possible while staying sustainable. Draft starter list, not finalized: Ministry of the Carrot (food & agriculture), Ministry of the Aubergine (water & resources), Ministry of the Sunflower (energy), Ministry of the Oak (land, forestry, housing), Ministry of the Bee (labor & population), Ministry of the Mushroom (waste & recycling — natural home for the recycling plant), Ministry of the Stag (enforcement/defense, tied to the central stag figurehead). The most recent brainstorm surfaced a wider pool of candidates leaning away from an all-vegetable naming scheme — wind, sun, water/"stuff" (a catch-all trash/upcycling ministry), biodiversity/insects/bees, and a "Ministry of Social Sustainability" built around a running who-can-be-most-dramatic-about-the-end-of-the-world bit. Current thinking favors capping vegetable-named ministries at one or two rather than stacking Carrot and Aubergine both, and landing on roughly ten ministries total (twenty guests each, at \~200 guests). Full candidate list and the final call live in the committee working doc.

**The stag.** Central figurehead — a "Father Time"-style pagan mother-nature figure combining wisdom with ruthlessness. Everyone jockeys for its favor.

**No assigned elite tier.** Guests are not sorted into an "elite" faction at the door — that idea has been dropped. The world can still have elites narratively (in-character officials, performers), but it isn't something a guest gets assigned into.

# **Games & activities**

A working list from the most recent brainstorm — organizers and creator volunteers will pick these up, adapt them, or replace them with their own; nothing here is locked. The Ecotopia app's Creators section (see the app build prompt) is the intended place to hand this list to volunteers along with the real, out-of-voice design context.

●       **Wig snatching.** Two people in wigs try to snatch each other's; fast rounds, easy to run as a tournament if it catches on. A possible framing: the wig as a stand-in for free thought/free speech.

●       **Sound guessing.** One guest, back turned, with several people behind them; each gets a themed prompt ("a tired bear," "the last bee on Earth," "a lion finally caged in a zoo") and has to make that sound, and the guesser has to work out who made which noise. Prompts leaning into the sustainability/fascism duality.

●       **How the world ends.** Guests compete to come up with the most dramatic possible telling of how the world ends — a speech, a song, a short theater piece, whatever form they want, as long as it's expressed.

●       **Rally speech.** A stage, a mic, banners, a semi-private space to actually be heard. Guests write and deliver a rally-style speech pushing their ministry's policies, ideally with a signature hand gesture, in the spirit of getting a crowd enthusiastic about joining.

●       **Confession booth.** Guests confess the worst thing they've done to the planet — deliberately just that one prompt, kept simple.

●       **Propaganda posters / mural.** An art-making station for posters or a mural — flagged as possibly too much effort for some guests, so needs a genuinely low-friction on-ramp if it stays in.

●       **Becoming nature.** A quieter, immersive room — plants, bird sound, guests learning to move like plants or butterflies, trying to connect with nature rather than compete. Scoring mechanism still undecided (maybe guests judge each other).

●       **Policy infiltration.** A whisper-down-the-line style game where a guest has to learn another ministry's policy and "improve" (i.e. corrupt) it, passed along and mangled the way a game of telephone would — deliberately silly rather than an open-ended policy-writing exercise, since nobody wants guests actually sitting down to draft serious sustainability policy.

●       **Poaching.** Ministries try to poach each other's members.

●       **Planting on people.** A subtle, ongoing prank game — plant a sticker or small object on someone without them noticing; earlier precedent at other parties was literally planting potatoes on people. A candidate task for guests seeded toward the resistance specifically.

●       **Lights off.** A chaotic, silly game built around turning lights on and off to save electricity — guests running around the venue trying to enforce or dodge it.

●       **Recycled glasses check.** A tiny recurring interaction rather than a full game: bar/cleanup volunteers ask guests "has this been recycled?" about their glass as a bit, tied into glass-recycling cleanup duty.

Ideas floated but not adopted as separate mechanics: a literal "state police" actor layer confiscating drinks and handing out fines was discussed and set aside for now as adding too much on top of the revolution mechanic already in play — it may still show up as pure visual atmosphere (people in uniform periodically doing something visibly ominous) without becoming its own interactive system. Explicit "murder" as a punishment mechanic was also discussed and dropped in favor of the recycling-plant upcycling ritual above, both to keep the tone right and because it fits the "everything is worded positively" rule much better.

# **Atmosphere: always watched**

Cameras are visible throughout the venue, so guests feel watched everywhere, all the time. Paired with the existing sound installation — hallway speakers running unsettling, posh-accented announcements processed like an old radio broadcast — except the voices don't just announce, they actively instruct guests to do specific things in the moment. Between the two, the surveillance is meant to feel responsive, not just ambient. A possible addition from the latest session: a short in-character "onboarding" induction video, in the style of Lexi's earlier propaganda film work, playing somewhere in the entrance sequence before guests reach registration.

# **Voice and messaging**

Ecotopia is never called the "Eco-Fascist Party" anywhere public — the public framing is a hopeful, magical-sounding utopia, told entirely from inside its own voice. That same positivity rule runs all the way down to individual rules and punishments, not just headline propaganda.

Two propaganda pieces are needed now: a save-the-date / invite poster telling people to apply for Ecotopia (guest-facing), and a separate recruitment poster aimed at pulling in people to help design and run games and activities inside the party. Both are distinct from the existing "PVV — Partij voor de Volgzaamheid" poster, which is organizer-recruitment branding for the outreach committee, not guest-facing. The in-universe ruling party's name is decided: **R.E.I.G.N.** (Regime for Ecological Integrity and Global Nurturing) — see the committee working doc for how that was chosen.
